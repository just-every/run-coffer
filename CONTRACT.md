# Coffer contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Coffer is the nonprofit fundraising CRM a US nonprofit buys instead of Bloomerang, Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT, Little Green Light or Neon CRM, and instead of collecting gifts through Givebutter or Zeffy: donor and household relationships, online and recurring giving, gift processing and receipts, appeals and stewardship journeys that are actually sent, major-gift moves management, and retention reporting reconciled with the books. It stays its own product; neighboring products the market sells separately are connected, not rebuilt.

This is a target contract: the product Coffer is being built towards, not a description of today's release. Every named clause states the behavior a buyer gets and the acceptance evidence that proves it through a public door. The [Status](#status) table marks how close the current code is. The nine released clauses keep their IDs and guarantees; where the wider scope changed them (sending, live payments, agent grants), the change is written into the clause.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Nonprofit donor management / fundraising CRM |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Bloomerang, Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT, Little Green Light, Neon CRM, Givebutter, Zeffy |
| First buyer | A US 501(c)(3) development lead with 500 to 20,000 donor records and a fundraising team of one to ten, moving from Little Green Light or Bloomerang; amounts in USD, IRS receipt rules, the nonprofit's own Cloudflare and Stripe accounts |

Givebutter and Zeffy are the up-and-comers: they bundle collection with a donor CRM at no platform fee. Coffer has to beat them on giving and the incumbents on records, stewardship and reporting. Switching means a buyer of any of them finds the whole job:

- **Know every supporter.** People and organizations with name parts, salutations and envelope names, several emails, phones and addresses each, households and relationships, a timeline of every touch, custom fields on records, gifts and interactions, duplicates merged, saved lists with bulk actions, documents, verified addresses, volunteer history, an active, inactive or deceased status every send honors, and a do-not-solicit restriction every ask honors while receipts and statements still go (`DONOR-001` to `DONOR-015`).
- **Record every gift exactly.** An immutable ledger with batch entry, attribution, splits, pledges with installments, soft credits, matching, tribute, in-kind, stock, DAF and IRA gifts, and anonymous gifts with recognition names (`GIFT-001` to `GIFT-011`).
- **Take gifts online and by phone.** Forms, campaign pages, recurring giving, card, ACH, wallets, PayPal and Venmo, staff-entered card and bank payments, a donor portal, failed-payment recovery, fraud protection and text-to-give; campaigns, events with auction results, peer-to-peer pages and memberships (`GIVE-*`, `CAMP-*`).
- **Reconcile with the books.** Live Stripe, other giving platforms and shop orders with payouts, fees, refunds and disputes, posted by fund to QuickBooks Online or Pantry and reconciled to the cent (`PAY-*`, `FIN-*`).
- **Thank and receipt.** Immediate email receipts, delivered acknowledgments, print-ready letters, year-end statements, one piece per household, an acknowledgment status on every gift and soft credit that imports carry and staff can set by hand, every template merging names, salutations and custom fields, US receipt rules and delivery tracking (`ACK-001`, `RCPT-*`).
- **Reach donors and hear back.** Consent on every channel, no ask to a donor marked do not solicit, appeals sent through Mango or Mailchimp, one-to-one email, replies routed to the right person, SMS and direct mail, with results (`CONSENT-001`, `APPEAL-001`, `COMM-*`).
- **Follow up.** Tasks, recurring tasks, stewardship journeys, pledge reminders, gift-triggered rules and meetings (`STEW-*`), and the major-gift pipeline with portfolios, plans, scores, research, grants and planned giving (`MAJOR-*`).
- **Report.** Retention, LYBUNT and SYBUNT, dashboards, a report builder, scheduled reports, recurring health, Form 990 support and giving by state (`REPORT-*`).
- **Move in and own it.** Importers from Little Green Light, Bloomerang, Raiser's Edge NXT, Neon CRM, Givebutter, Zeffy and any spreadsheet, custom fields and name fields included, with a step-by-step switching guide and a checked cutover, monthly donors moved with their cards and bank accounts; roles, SSO and an audit log; an API, webhooks and MCP; phone and native apps; and free, open-source ownership with exports and erasure (`MOVE-*`, `TEAM-*`, `API-*`, `MOBILE-*`, `DATA-*`, `OWN-*`).
- **Go further.** A development agent that reconciles every gift, rescues failed monthly gifts, sends missing receipts, drafts stewardship from the nonprofit's own history and shows who needs a human call next, under named grants (`DEV-001` to `DEV-012`).

### How to read this contract

Each clause is `AREA-NNN — Title.`, then the behavior, its *Acceptance* (an HTTP route under `/api/coffer`, the browser at 1440 and 390 pixels, a donor-facing page, a provider webhook, a native app, or an import or export file) and its status in the [Status](#status) table. `done` means the code implements the clause and a cited test exercises it; `partial` means part of it ships, with the cited file or test and the gap; `missing` means no code yet. Fakes are allowed only at a paid or network edge (Stripe, PayPal, QuickBooks Online, Pantry, Mango, Mailchimp, Twilio, mail delivery, Smarty, DonorSearch, a model); a provider fake proves Coffer's side only, never live provider behavior, and local checks never prove hosted deployment. The release gate is [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md).

## Released guarantees

The first release's clauses, kept and widened where the target changed them.

#### HTTP-001 — Private app boundary.

Hono serves `/health` and owns `/api`; native owner/operator sessions protect `/app` and `/api/coffer`. Every handler checks the actor's role, the exact host and, for cookie changes, the same `Origin`. Each write batch re-checks the session or key inside the same D1 transaction, so a revoked session, role change or revoked key stops a request in flight. Unknown paths return real 404s. Public donor-facing pages added by the target (`GIVE-001`, `GIVE-005`, `CAMP-002`) live outside `/app` under their own rules and never widen this boundary.

*Acceptance:* The role-boundary test signs in as owner, member and agent, sends cross-origin writes, revokes a key and deactivates a member, and every refused request returns 401 or 403.

#### DONOR-001 — Donors and households.

A donor has an email (or none) that no unrelated record shares at a staff add/edit write. DONOR-001 refuses an unrelated shared email at write time (staff add/edit); the duplicates queue (DONOR-006) takes the doors that cannot refuse synchronously (import, form, merge). A household is found by name or created. Several emails, phones and postal addresses per constituent with one primary of each, and an email shared by members of one household, are `DONOR-013`; a person's name parts, salutations and envelope name are `DONOR-014`. Members, admins and the owner add and edit donors; edits are versioned, and a stale version is refused rather than overwritten.

*Acceptance:* A member adds donors through HTTP and the browser journey adds, edits and opens donors; the donor page lists household members.

#### GIFT-001 — Immutable ledger.

A gift is an integer-cent line with its date, method, fund, optional pledge and goods-or-services answer. Database triggers refuse changes to amounts, dates or references. Reassigning a recorded gift to another donor is prohibited except through an audited donor merge (`DONOR-006`), undo of that merge, or erasure (`DATA-001`). Each exception preserves every monetary fact (amount, date, fund and receipt), records who did it and why, and is reversible where its clause says so. The guarded paths are `src/coffer/donor-merge.ts` and `src/coffer/http.ts`. A correction is a reversal line of the opposite amount, once per gift. Dollar input is parsed exactly; the API takes `<field>Cents` for exact integers. Every later gift kind in this contract (online, recurring, split, soft-credited, in-kind, refunded) is written as lines in this one ledger. In-kind originals and linked correction-history lines have explicit noncash kinds and zero monetary contribution cents; their donor-stated internal values are separate fields, never cash amounts. Their receipt dates, IDs and funds are immutable, and current property metadata projects the append-only history. Monetary reports and receipts use only monetary kinds; the donor API exposes the unified ledger under the same donor-read authority.

*Acceptance:* The ledger test records exact-cent gifts, refuses sub-cent, future-dated and duplicate-reference gifts, reverses one once and reads the lifetime and per-year totals back. An HTTP journey refuses direct donor reassignment, merges two donors, undoes the merge and erases a donor; after each permitted reassignment it compares amount, date, fund and receipt with the originals and reads the actor and reason in the audit record. Undo restores gift ownership; erasure removes personal data and makes merge undo unavailable. The clause-named GIFT-001/GIFT-009 journey reads noncash originals and correction history from that same donor ledger, refuses a changed receipt date, compares original numeric/date/fund facts across correction, merge, undo and erasure, and asserts no cash report or receipt is created.

#### CONSENT-001 — Consent history.

Consent is append-only per donor and channel with method, evidence, capture date and who recorded it; the newest row is the current answer. Rows are removed only by erasure. The target adds SMS and phone channels, donor self-service changes and sender-synced unsubscribes as further rows of this same history (`COMM-001`).

*Acceptance:* The appeal test records grants and withdrawals for several donors and channels and the donor history shows the newest answer per channel.

#### ACK-001 — Acknowledgment letters.

Gifts at or over the threshold (default $250) without a live letter are due within the target days (default 14). No letter is prepared until a person has saved the settings; the letter must state donor name, amount, gift date, organization and the goods-or-services statement. A batch freezes its letters and content hash. A person approves the exact hash; export, then a recorded sent date. Imported gifts with an unknown goods answer wait for a person's answer. `RCPT-011` and the importers widen this: an imported gift takes its goods answer from the source's non-deductible amount (`MOVE-001` to `MOVE-006`), so only a gift whose source holds no answer waits; and a gift is due only while its acknowledgment status is "not acknowledged", so a gift acknowledged in Coffer or in the incumbent before the move, marked do not acknowledge, or received before the acknowledge-from date (`MOVE-007`) is never due or overdue, and recording a batch's sent date marks each of its gifts acknowledged by letter with the batch linked. This is the reviewed letter path for printed and hand-mailed acknowledgments; delivering the same approved letters by email is `RCPT-002`, print layouts are `RCPT-003` and one letter per household is `RCPT-009`.

The existing LGL, Bloomerang and NXT export doors carry source acknowledgment facts and exclude acknowledged and do-not-acknowledge gifts from this queue. Source dates and methods are retained when supplied; missing historical details stay unrecorded. A recorded batch sent date stores acknowledged status, letter method, date and exact batch/letter links on each gift atomically. MOVE-004, MOVE-005 and MOVE-006 inherit these status and goods-answer obligations before adding their import doors.

*Acceptance:* The acknowledgment test has an agent prepare a batch, refuses preparation before settings are reviewed, lets only a person approve, exports the CSV with the exact letter text and records the sent date. It checks imported goods values, excludes gifts imported as acknowledged or do not acknowledge and gifts before the acknowledge-from date, and reads each sent gift as acknowledged by letter with its batch linked.

#### APPEAL-001 — Consent-checked appeals.

An audience includes only donors whose newest consent for the channel is yes and who have the needed address; exclusions are counted by reason. Each recipient is frozen with the exact consent row; approval, export and sending refuse if any of those rows is no longer current. Sending the approved audience through Mango or Mailchimp is `COMM-002`; this clause is the consent gate every send passes. Excluding inactive and deceased constituents is `DONOR-012`; excluding constituents with a solicitation restriction under the reason "do not solicit", and deliberately including one in a run, is `DONOR-015`; and one piece per household is `RCPT-009`.

*Acceptance:* The appeal test prepares an audience, counts exclusions by reason, refuses approval after a withdrawal and exports only consenting recipients.

#### AGENT-001 — Prepare, then a person decides.

An expiring, revocable bearer key reads records and runs only `ack.prepare` and `appeal.prepare`. Approvals keep the preparer's and approver's identity and role with the content hash. Wider, separately granted agent work is `DEV-012`; no agent key gains an action this clause does not name unless that grant says so.

*Acceptance:* The role-boundary and acknowledgment tests create a key, prepare with it, are refused every other command, record agent and owner roles on the approval and stop working on revocation.

#### IMPORT-001 — Reviewed Stripe move-in.

Saved Stripe customer and charge list pages go through Base's import kit. Preview writes nothing; apply needs the exact reviewed fingerprint; the journal retains the original pages; undo stops at anything a person has since built on. Failed and non-USD charges are skipped with reasons and never enter the plan. A partially refunded charge imports at its net amount in integer cents, with gross, refunded and net cents recorded beside it. A fully refunded charge imports as refunded with net zero and is never silently dropped. If uploaded IDs repeat within the file or match already-imported records, preview rejects the entire upload, names every duplicate ID and row, and imports nothing until the owner fixes the file. This saved-page path stays for history a live connection (`PAY-001`) is not granted, and for nonprofits that never give Coffer API access.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP move-in journey previews failed and non-USD skips, a partially refunded charge with gross 10,000, refunded 2,500 and net 7,500 cents, and a fully refunded charge with gross and refunded 10,000 cents and net zero retained as refunded. It rejects an upload with repeated IDs within the file and one with already-imported IDs, names every ID and row, and verifies that neither preview imports anything; the owner fixes the file before apply. It refuses a wrong fingerprint, applies the exact reviewed plan, reads the original pages and refund facts in the journal and undoes; a second journey proves undo stops at a donor a person has since changed.

#### DATA-001 — Custody and erasure.

Base portability backs up product records, approvals and import custody, excluding identity and credentials, and restores only into an empty installation. A hosted Coffer backs up and restores the same archive with `rer run backup:hosted`, authenticated by the owner's session; its restore goes only into a newly deployed, empty environment and keeps that environment's own owner. The owner erases a donor with explicit `ERASE`: personal data, consent, letter text, gift notes and affected Stripe originals go; gifts move to an anonymous placeholder; hashed suppression stops re-import; a later journal fences older archives. Every record the target adds (interactions, tasks, opportunities, receipts, provider events) joins the same backup and the same erasure.

*Acceptance:* The backup test restores into a fresh installation and compares donor histories and approvals; the hosted-restore test backs up through the command, restores into a fresh installation with large retained originals, keeps the destination owner and refuses a second restore; the erasure tests remove personal data, keep anonymous gifts, refuse re-import and fence an older archive.

## Target clauses

These clauses complete the category job alongside the released guarantees.

### Supporter records

The constituent record a Little Green Light or Bloomerang user expects on day one: people, organizations, the ties between them and every touch.

#### DONOR-002 — Organization records.

A constituent is a person or an organization (business, foundation, church, school, government body, donor-advised fund sponsor). An organization has its own address, website, EIN and giving history, and named contacts who are person records linked to it with a role. Gifts from an organization credit the organization; its contacts see the gift on their timeline as related. An organization's letters and receipts are addressed to the organization with "Attn:" and its primary named contact, and open with that contact's salutation (`DONOR-014`). An organization counts by its own address in the by-state report (`REPORT-008`).

Current organization records and contact links use the existing staff donor-write permission and version checks. A primary contact is explicitly selected, never inferred. The organization's postal address and email remain the destinations; its display name plus "Attn:" and the primary contact name form the addressee. The greeting uses that person's explicit informal salutation, then nickname, first name and full display name, in that order. Without a primary contact the organization name remains the greeting and addressee. Current acknowledgment batches, missing-receipt batches, online receipts, corrected receipts, annual statements and property acknowledgments share this addressing; expanded `DONOR-014` name defaults and future `RCPT-010` template doors inherit it before shipping. Contact timelines label organization gifts as related only, outside the contact's own gifts, giving totals and tax receipts. Contact links and organization details survive archive/restore and merge/undo; erasure removes links and redacts a contact's names from frozen organization documents.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP and browser journey creates a foundation with two contacts, records a $10,000 grant payment from it, and reads the gift on the foundation, not the contacts, with both contacts listing it as related; the grant's receipt is addressed to the foundation, attention its primary contact, and opens with that contact's informal salutation. The organization's gifts appear under its own state in the `REPORT-008` report.

#### DONOR-003 — Relationships.

Any two constituents can be related with a reciprocal type (spouse/spouse, parent/child, employee/employer, board member/organization, friend, advisor/client) that shows from both sides. Households keep a head of household, a formal and informal salutation and an addressee for letters, computed from the members' name parts (`DONOR-014`) by an organization-wide default ("Dr. Robert and Mrs. Jane Smith", "Bob and Jane") and overridable per household.

The current computation joins nonempty prefix, first, middle, last and suffix with spaces, falling back to the existing display name when no parts exist. Informal computation uses nickname, then first name, then display name. Members are joined with " and "; an explicitly selected living head comes first, followed by record creation then ID. Organization formats accept `{{full_names}}`, `{{formal_names}}`, `{{first_names}}` and `{{last_names}}`; defaults use full names for addressee/formal and first names for informal.

Only explicit staff selection sets a head: a current member who is a living person, neither erased nor merged away; inactive and do-not-solicit members remain eligible, organizations do not. "No head selected" is visible, valid and never blocks gift entry, totals or mailings. No head is guessed from first membership, age, largest giving, name order, title, gender or import row order. Head selection never changes or defaults hard or soft credits, pledges, matches, acknowledgment status, receipt recipients or legal receipt identity, consent, solicitation restrictions, recognition names, anonymity, portfolio, relationship manager, portal access or the member against whom a gift is entered, and never reassigns money. Today owner, admin and member may change the head and overrides; agents may read but writes return 403. Only owner/admin set organization-wide defaults, recomputing every non-overridden household with its affected count shown first. Before `TEAM-001` wider roles ship, fundraiser within portfolio and data entry may write; finance, volunteer and read-only are refused. This obligation covers every household capability that exists today. Before solicitation restrictions (`DONOR-015`), wider roles (`TEAM-001`), another importer (`MOVE-004` to `MOVE-006`) or another household mailing door (`RCPT-009`, `COMM-007`) ships, its clause inherits and asserts the same head eligibility, authority, version, money and snapshot rules; no future restriction can make a do-not-solicit person ineligible for headship.

New households have no head. Until selection, computed names use record creation order then ID; that ordering is never stored or shown as headship. `MOVE-001` to `MOVE-006` carry a head only from an explicit source flag, with source system and import date; inferred households and unflagged sources have no head. Source addressee and salutations stay unchanged as overrides where different from computation. Preview counts households with and without a head; import undo stops at any household a person has since changed.

Moving or removing a head clears the old household's head in the same change, assigns nobody and leaves gifts, credits, acknowledgments and receipts with that member. A deceased member remains a member, is omitted from computed names, and a deceased head is flagged, never automatically replaced; choosing a deceased head is refused. Merge carries headship to the survivor only if the survivor stays in that household, otherwise clears it; undo restores exactly or refuses if the household changed since, without changing the ledger. Erasure clears the erased donor's headship, all three household overrides and their name in the household name; their gifts move to the anonymous placeholder and leave household totals. No household action deletes a soft credit; this neither changes nor endorses `DATA-001`'s existing erasure deletion of soft credits.

Every household has a version. Head, override and membership writes check it and re-check actor authority and head eligibility in the same database transaction; stale writes return 409 without overwriting. Audit records retain actor, role, household ID, old and new head and which overrides were set or cleared. Prepared household mailing runs record household versions and refuse approval after any change. Addressee, formal and informal salutation independently use computation or an override. Overrides stay exactly as typed through head, name-part and membership changes; a membership change flags them for review at the next mailing, never silently clears them. Erasure is the only automatic clear; a person clearing an override returns that field to computation.

Household money sums net balances of distinct ledger gift IDs whose hard-credit donor, after any merge, is a current member: joint gifts count once, split gifts count once at net with fund views using allocations, reversals count zero, refunds reduce balances, and a won-dispute reinstatement counts once through its own processor entry. Anonymous gifts count in staff totals. Soft credits, in-kind values, open pledges, expected matches and gifts hard-credited to non-members (including employers, donor-advised fund sponsors and the erasure placeholder) are excluded. Gifts are never matched by amount, date, name, reference, method or fund: two equal same-day gifts both count. A separate labelled recognition view adds each outside gift soft-crediting members once, at the lesser of its remaining balance and those members' combined credits; reversals count zero. Soft credits remain on credited donors with their own acknowledgment status.

Totals use current membership at read time, including each current member's whole history before joining, labelled "current members as of <date>"; periods use gift received dates and the existing fiscal-year setting. Prepared and sent household statements freeze addressee, members, gift lines and total: later head, membership and override changes never alter, void or renumber them. Only financial changes (reversal, refund, reassignment or split change) void or correct under `RCPT-008`; erasure redacts under `DATA-001`. Each line stays under its hard-credit donor with existing deductible facts; soft credits never appear. This adds no tax or legal guarantee.

*Acceptance:* One clause-named HTTP journey relates an employer and spouse and reads reciprocity, then uses Robert and Jane Smith, Sam, a donor-advised fund sponsor and a duplicate Jane. In 2025 Robert has two distinct same-day $100 gifts A/A2; Jane has B, $200 split $120/$80; C is $1,000 hard-credited to Robert and soft-credited $1,000 to Jane; sponsor gift D is $500 with $500/$300 soft credits to Robert/Jane; Jane's in-kind laptop E has $900 stated value. A member creates a headless household with computed names, selects Jane and reads the audit; money is $1,400 with B's fund allocations, recognition separately $1,900, C once, A/A2 twice, and D/E excluded from money. Changing the head to Robert and overriding informal salutation to "Bob and Jane" preserves every gift, credit, acknowledgment and receipt and the exact override. An approved 2025 statement is one $1,400 piece with C once and no soft-credit line. Sam joins with a pre-membership 2024 $50 gift, increasing lifetime money $50, then leaves and it reverts; the statement stays frozen and the override is flagged. Reversing C gives money $400 and recognition $900, retains Jane's credit at zero and voids/corrects the statement under `RCPT-008`. Marking Robert deceased flags him without replacement, excludes him from computed names for confirmation and preserves the override; selecting Jane, merging duplicate Jane's $25 gift and undoing increases then restores totals without changing ledger facts. Erasing Jane clears headship, all overrides and her name in the household name and removes her gifts from totals under `DATA-001`. A Bloomerang flagged source head carries with provenance and source names unchanged; LGL inference has no head; previews count each. The journey refuses agent head/override writes (403), stale writes (409), non-member/deceased/erased/merged-away/organization heads, guessed heads, approval after a household version change and merge/import undo after a person's household edit. It checks same-transaction authority/eligibility, head movement clearing without replacement, independent override clearing, owner/admin-only recomputation of every non-overridden household with the affected count shown first, fixed headless name order, received-date/fiscal/current-membership labels, refund and reinstatement treatment, and that household actions do not delete soft credits. It compares original gift, credit, acknowledgment, receipt and prior audit facts throughout except the specified financial, merge/undo and erasure operations, reads each new household audit, and runs the household panel at 1440 and 390 pixels. It also asserts inactive/do-not-solicit head eligibility, visible headless gift entry/totals/mailings, and that head selection never changes or defaults pledges, matches, receipt recipients/legal identity, consent, solicitation restrictions, recognition/anonymity, portfolios, relationship managers, portal access or gift-entry donor. All three overrides independently survive head/name/member changes verbatim and clear to computation only by explicit action or erasure; membership changes flag them for the next mailing. Current members' whole prior history counts, anonymous gifts count in staff money, and open pledges, expected matches, outside employer/sponsor/erasure-placeholder gifts and in-kind values do not; no amount/date/name/reference/method/fund matching combines distinct IDs. Frozen statements retain hard-credit donor and deductible facts, and non-financial changes never renumber or void them; erasure redacts without adding a tax/legal guarantee. Before wider roles ship, `TEAM-001` asserts its household write matrix.

#### DONOR-004 — Interaction timeline.

Each constituent has one timeline of everything that happened: gifts, pledges, receipts sent and delivered, appeal sends, opens and replies, logged calls, meetings, visits, notes, tasks, event attendance and volunteer hours, newest first and filterable. Logging a call, meeting or note takes under ten seconds from the donor page or a phone: at most two taps or clicks plus the note text, with type, date and author defaulting to a call now by the signed-in person, and the entry shows on the timeline as soon as it is saved. Notes marked confidential are visible only to the roles `TEAM-001` allows. Today the owner and admins alone record and read confidential notes; the member role is data entry and is refused those notes through direct HTTP, donor/context reads and exports. The owner’s private archive retains them. Staff record ordinary notes and visits in the existing portable note store, while calls, meetings and email remain conversations; all share organization-calendar dates and default to today and the signed-in author. Call, meeting and note quick actions take two clicks plus typing. Timeline type and inclusive date filters apply to every current gift, pledge, receipt and provider-delivery event, appeal, reply, conversation, task and volunteer source, newest first. This obligation is complete for current sources. Before event attendance (`CAMP-002`), tracked opens (`RCPT-006`) or another activity source ships, its clause adds it to this timeline and its filters; `TEAM-001` must apply its future confidential-note role and portfolio rules to every read/context/export door before those roles ship.

*Acceptance:* A browser journey at 1440 and 390 pixels logs a call and a confidential note on a donor, counting at most two taps or clicks plus typing for each, and a timed run with a 30-character note typed at five characters a second takes under ten seconds from the first tap to the entry on the timeline; it sees both on the timeline with the gift and receipt events, filters the timeline to calls only and then to a date range and sees only the matching entries each time, and a data-entry user is refused the confidential note through HTTP.

#### DONOR-005 — Custom fields and tags.

An admin adds custom fields to constituents, gifts, pledges, opportunities, interactions and notes. Each field has a type (text, number, currency in exact cents, date, year, checkbox) and an entry method (free-form, pick-one or pick-many from a pick list the admin edits). An admin also defines tags and categories (board member, volunteer, alumni, legacy society). A value is checked against its type and pick list wherever it enters: the record page, batch entry, a form question (`GIVE-001`), the API and every import. Fields and tags are searchable, segmentable in saved lists (`DONOR-007`), usable as report columns and groupings (`REPORT-004`), mergeable into every template (`RCPT-010`) and exportable, and the importers fill them from the incumbent's own custom fields (`MOVE-010`). Renaming a field or a pick-list value keeps every stored value.

New fields join their type's `DATA-002` column set when they ship.

Current record, gift, batch, command/API and LGL/Bloomerang/configured NXT CSV/XLSX entry doors share the typed validator. Source custom columns require explicit reviewed mappings and retain their original source record, column and value with replay and guarded undo. Categories and versioned choice renames are owner/admin-only; a rename moves current values and saved filters while preserving earlier history and prepared documents. #### GIFT-003 — Campaign, appeal, fund and channel attribution.

Every gift carries a fund (designation), a campaign, an appeal and its source (form, event, mail piece, email, text-to-give) as structured references, not free text. Funds are restricted or unrestricted with an accounting mapping (`FIN-003`). Online gifts take these from the form and tracked link; imported gifts from the source mapping.

The fund is the gift's ledger fund, named as in `FIN-003` (a name without a definition is an unrestricted fund), and each split part (`GIFT-004`) carries its own fund and campaign. The campaign is a Coffer campaign by id and must raise for the gift's own fund. The appeal is a defined appeal referenced by its code (matched without regard to letter case and stored as defined); the owner or an admin defines appeals with a permanent code and a name, and an entry naming an undefined appeal is refused. The source is one of five channels (giving form, event, mail piece, email, text-to-give) or "Channel not recorded". Campaign, appeal and channel are optional on staff entry; a gift without one counts under "No campaign", "No appeal" or "Channel not recorded", never a guessed value. ### Campaigns, events and memberships

The fundraising programs the incumbents run gifts through.

#### CAMP-001 — Campaigns with goals.

Giving donors and gifts have a positive remaining campaign balance; new donors made their first remaining gift in Coffer through the campaign or a nested phase. ### Payments

Gifts, fees, refunds and payouts arrive from the processor by themselves and match to the cent.

#### PAY-001 — Live Stripe connection.

The owner connects the nonprofit's Stripe account (Stripe Connect OAuth or a restricted key). Coffer registers a signed webhook, processes each event once by its id, backfills history through the list APIs, and runs a daily sweep of balance transactions that catches any missed event. Payments made outside Coffer (Payment Links, Stripe Checkout elsewhere, an existing subscription) become gifts matched to donors by email, with unclear matches waiting in a review queue.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP journey with Stripe in test mode connects an account, receives charge and refund webhooks (one replayed, one with a bad signature refused), drops one event and sees the daily sweep add it, and reviews an ambiguous donor match.

#### PAY-002 — Payouts with their gifts.

Each Stripe payout is recorded with every gift, refund, dispute and fee it contains, so a bank deposit opens to the exact gifts behind it. A payout whose contents do not sum to the deposited amount is flagged with the difference explained.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP journey with the Stripe edge faked receives a payout of 14 charges, two fees and one refund, opens it in Coffer, and reads gross, fees, refunds and net equal to the deposit.

#### PAY-003 — Refunds and disputes.

A refund or dispute from the processor (or a staff-initiated refund through Coffer, which calls Stripe) writes a reversal line against the original gift, voids or corrects its receipt (`RCPT-008`), notifies the relationship manager, and for a dispute tracks evidence due dates and the outcome. A won dispute reinstates the gift as a new line.

Staff refunds use the existing owner/admin payment-management boundary and exact positive USD cents. The request freezes the gift's current remaining contribution and a permanent attempt identity before one Stripe call; a changed balance, oversized refund or unresolved earlier attempt refuses a new request. Replays return the saved attempt without another call. Pending, failed and uncertain responses never count as completed refunds; only successful provider balance entries adjust contributions. A successful adjustment voids affected receipts atomically under `RCPT-008`; staff prepare a corrected replacement for the remaining gift and use ordinary human approval before sending it.

The relationship manager follows the existing reply-routing order: active portfolio manager, active opportunity owner, then active installation owner. Each refund, dispute withdrawal and reinstatement has one durable notice attempt through the installation's SendGrid connection, with accepted, unavailable or uncertain outcomes visible. Provider acceptance is labelled submitted, never delivered, and replay never resends a notice. Disputes retain the current Stripe status, evidence deadline as an exact UTC instant, and the provider's evidence/past-due flags (unknown if absent); staff follow a link to review and submit evidence in Stripe. Wins reinstate contributions only through the processor's new balance entry, never by editing the original gift. Archive/restore preserves requests, deadlines and notice attempts without resending; gift custody follows merge/undo and erasure without storing donor text in these records.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP journey with the Stripe edge faked refunds half a gift, opens a dispute on another and wins it, and reads the reversal lines, the corrected receipt and the reinstated gift. The PAY-003 journey also asserts staff half-refund and pending/failed/uncertain outcomes, exact-cent and stale-balance refusals, durable attempt and webhook replay behavior, relationship-manager notice routing and acceptance, dispute evidence deadlines/statuses, unchanged original gifts and receipt correction approval. #### PAY-004 — Processing fees and fee cover.

Each online gift records its processing fee from the processor's balance transaction. A donor who covers the fee gives the grossed-up amount and is receipted for the full amount paid. Reports show gross, fees and net by fund, form and campaign.

For current hosted campaign forms, fee cover is optional and unchecked. The owner or admin enables it with an organization-supplied estimate: integer basis points and fixed cents, labelled as an estimate rather than a Stripe-verified rate. The charged integer-cent total is `ceil((requested cents + fixed cents) * 10000 / (10000 - rate basis points))`; it is shown before checkout, rechecked by the server and frozen with the request. Existing requests and monthly plans keep their agreed amounts when settings change. Receipts include that full paid total; reports use the processor’s actual fee, independently of the estimate. Gross, fees and net include signed refunds and adjustments, allocate split gifts by the existing exact-cent payout rules, and group by the recorded fund, original form and campaign; absent provenance stays explicitly unrecorded. Each grouping reconciles to the same transaction totals and payout net, with unmatched transactions and deposit differences visible.

The estimate uses one same-origin script with a fresh per-response nonce and no inline-script allowance, network requests or personal-data egress.

GIVE-001 independent forms, GIVE-003 added recurring schedules, GIVE-009 staff payment entry and PAY-005/PAY-006 additional giving providers inherit PAY-004 actual fee recording, honest fee-cover totals and actual form/campaign provenance before shipping.

### Accounting

Coffer reconciles to the books; the general ledger stays in Pantry or QuickBooks Online.

#### FIN-001 — QuickBooks Online sync.

The owner connects QuickBooks Online and maps each fund to an income account and class (or location), fees to an expense account, and the Stripe and checking accounts. Coffer posts one deposit per payout and per check batch with lines per fund and a fee line, so each deposit matches the bank feed; refunds and disputes post as their own entries. Posts are idempotent, a failed post retries and is shown, and nothing posts twice.

#### FIN-003 — Restricted funds.

Funds are marked restricted (purpose or time) or unrestricted; restricted gifts carry the restriction to accounting, and a release-of-restriction report shows what was given, spent (entered or synced from accounting) and still held per restricted fund. A time-restricted fund's remaining balance releases on its stored release date; a purpose-restricted fund releases as dated qualifying spending is recorded against it. The report is cumulative through the selected date and shows, per restricted fund, given (net of refunds), spent, released and still held. #### FIN-004 — Journal export for other ledgers.

An incomplete or mismatched deposit refuses the whole download with the reason named. *Acceptance:* An HTTP journey downloads a month's journal in CSV and IIF, checks that every recorded deposit in the period is included with its account and class mapping, and checks its totals by account against the ledger. #### FIN-005 — Reconciliation report.

For any period Coffer shows gifts in the ledger, money received by the processor, payouts deposited and amounts posted to accounting side by side by fund, with every difference listed and explained (pending ACH, unposted batch, refund after payout).

Dates are inclusive: gifts and processor entries use their recorded organization-local day; payouts use their recorded arrival day. Processing ACH is shown separately as pending, never counted as received cash. Differences are signed left-column minus right-column amounts with source IDs: offline gifts and batches, actual fees, later refunds, money outside the deposit period, unposted journals and incomplete payout membership. #### RCPT-005 — US receipt rules.

Receipt templates must state the organization's name and EIN, the amount or a description of non-cash property, the date, and whether goods or services were provided with their good-faith value. Coffer applies the $250 written-acknowledgment rule, the quid pro quo disclosure for any payment over $75 that is partly a contribution, the token-benefit exception at the IRS's current inflation-adjusted figures (a setting), and intangible religious benefit wording for religious organizations.

The receipt queue uses the lower of the configured threshold and $250 per individual payment, and also includes every partly deductible payment strictly over $75 with a positive goods value. A saved monetary template must include the organization’s EIN as well as its other required facts; goods wording always includes the limitation to the payment’s excess over the good-faith benefit value. Token exceptions require reviewed limits for the gift’s own calendar year, confirmation that these are the only benefits, and either fundraising-campaign fair value no more than both 2% of the gift and the reviewed dollar cap, or name/logo items whose aggregate charity cost and minimum gift meet the reviewed limits. The 2026 limits are $139, $13.90 and $69.50 respectively ([IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 §4.33](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)); missing or different-year limits refuse the exception. Intangible religious benefits require the reviewed exclusively religious organization setting and state no benefit value. Each exception freezes its actual benefit facts, rule and settings version beside the gift; the monetary ledger’s goods cents remain the deductible reduction (zero for an allowed exception). Individual, agent-prepared and annual receipts use that frozen basis. Backup/restore, merge/undo and erasure retain the same financial custody; erasure removes benefit descriptions and receipt text. A property acknowledgment uses the property description rather than its donor-stated dollar value.

#### RCPT-007 — Thank-you templates.

Admins keep several reviewed templates: per form and campaign, first gift, recurring start, major gift, tribute, in-kind and year-end. Each template is checked for the required receipt facts before it can be used, merges the fields `RCPT-010` lists, and its version is recorded with every message sent.

#### RCPT-008 — Corrected and voided receipts.

When a gift is reversed, refunded, split differently or reassigned, its receipt is voided or reissued with a corrected copy that says what changed, and the donor is told when a corrected receipt is sent. Receipt numbers are sequential per year and never reused.

Current individual, automatic online, annual and property documents share one sequence per UTC preparation year. A number is reserved when the document is prepared and is never reused after discard, voiding, erasure or restore. Existing documents receive numbers in preparation order during upgrade. A financial adjustment, reversal, reassignment or property correction voids affected originals atomically, including every annual statement containing the changed gift; their numbers, frozen wording and delivery history remain as historical copies labelled VOID. Split allocations are immutable: changing a split uses reversal and a new gift, voiding the original receipt. The owner or an admin can prepare a corrected individual receipt from the remaining ledger balance; it names its predecessor and the change, retains the original goods value and reviewed benefit facts, and goes through the existing exact-content human approval and delivery path. A fully refunded contribution, an annual statement with changed facts, or a remaining contribution consumed by its original benefit value stays void rather than claiming a deductible replacement. Corrected property acknowledgments retain their predecessor and correction reason. A correction notice is part of the corrected copy; preparation alone never claims notification or delivery. Before GIFT-010, GIVE-009 or any new receipt, reassignment or split-edit door ships, it inherits the same numbering, atomic invalidation and correction custody.

*Acceptance:* An HTTP journey refunds half a receipted gift and reads a corrected receipt with the next number referencing the original, and a voided one for a fully reversed gift. #### RCPT-009 — One piece per household.
