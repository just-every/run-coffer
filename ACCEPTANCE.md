# First-release acceptance

A development lead at a US nonprofit can, in the first release:

1. Move in a year of gifts from saved Stripe customer and charge pages: preview without changes, apply the exact reviewed plan, read its journal and undo it.
2. See each donor's history: gifts by year as exact ledger lines (corrections are reversals), pledges, consent, letters and appeals.
3. Send acknowledgements for gifts of $250 or more on time: an agent or person prepares letters in the charity's own wording, a person approves, exports them for their own mail and records when they went out.
4. Record an appeal only to donors whose current consent for that channel is yes; a change in consent stops approval and export.
5. Keep custody: back up and restore into an empty installation, and erase a donor's personal data without losing the gift ledger or reviving them from an old backup or a later import.

Every approval records who prepared and who approved the exact content. Roles are enforced on every server request.

Not needed: payment processing, online donation forms, sending email or mail, grant management, wealth screening, tax advice, non-cash gift valuation, receipts outside the US, a live Stripe connection, brand or homepage design, docs site or the public run site (waits for the domain).

Local checks do not prove hosted deployment; the independent stranger journey is run separately.
